## Week 2 (24–30 Sep 2026)

- **Physics layer convention** — named the 3D physics layers in `project.godot` (`World`, `Marble`, `Hazards`, `Triggers`) and set the marble to layer `Marble`, colliding with `World` and `Hazards`. Documented the full per-object layer/mask table in [tile-convention.md §6](tile-convention.md), including for hazards and triggers Andrew hasn't built yet, so the convention is settled before more collision-bearing scenes exist.
- **Isometric camera follow rig** — built `CameraRig.cs`/`CameraRig.tscn`: a rig that tracks the marble's position with frame-rate-independent exponential smoothing (`1 - exp(-k·delta)`, not a fixed-alpha `Lerp`) while holding a fixed pitch/yaw/distance offset on its `Camera3D` child, so the view stays a consistent isometric angle rather than a generic third-person follow-cam. Exposed pitch, yaw, distance, follow speed and orthographic size as tunable exports. Diagnosed and fixed a case where `Distance` appeared to do nothing once the camera was switched to orthographic projection — under Orthogonal, translation along the view axis doesn't change apparent zoom, so added a separate `OrthographicSize` export driving `Camera3D.Size`, the property that actually controls zoom in that mode. Replaced the static test-scene `Camera3D` in `Main.tscn` with this rig. Satisfies the smoothed, isometric camera requirement (requirements.md §3.2). Developed on `feature/camera-follow`.
- **Checkpoint and respawn system** — added a `CheckpointManager` autoload (`scripts/Core/`) tracking the marble's current respawn point, a reusable `Checkpoint` trigger that updates it, and a `MarbleController.Die()` entry point that resets the marble's transform/velocity to the last checkpoint. `KillZone` (a large trigger below the level) and any future hazard both call `Die()` the same way — via `body.Call("Die")` duck-typing where only a `Node3D` reference is available — so hazard code Andrew writes later needs no dependency on `MarbleController`. Checkpoint/kill-zone triggers live on the `Triggers` physics layer, masked to `Marble` only. Added visible placeholder markers (translucent CSG box + material) so the trigger volumes aren't invisible while building. This overlaps with Andrew's owned checkpoint/respawn scope (project-plan-responsibilities.md §1) — built now to unblock testing, flagged for his review. Developed on `feature/checkpoint-respawn`.
- **Win state** — added a `GameStateManager` autoload signalling `LevelWon`/`LevelLost` (the latter unraised for now, reserved for the timer's lose condition so that system can plug in later without further changes here), a `Goal` trigger (same Triggers-layer pattern as `Checkpoint`/`KillZone`) that calls `Win()`, and a placeholder `WinMessage` UI that shows "YOU WIN" on the signal. `MarbleController` stops responding to input once the level ends. This is scaffolding across two other owners' scope (Andrew's win/lose, Jayden's UI, per project-plan-responsibilities.md §1) — built minimally so the game feel work isn't blocked, and flagged for their review. Developed on `feature/win-state`.
- **Lose state: countdown timer, game over screen and retry** — added `LevelTimer` (per-level countdown that calls `GameStateManager.Lose()` at zero and freezes on win or lose), a `TimerDisplay` HUD readout driven by the timer's `TimeChanged` signal (the timer owns only the time and never references the UI), a placeholder `GameOverMessage` screen with a retry button, and `GameStateManager.Retry()`, which clears the ended state and reloads the current scene so the marble, checkpoint, timer and HUD all reset. Debugged a bug where falling as the timer hit zero let the marble respawn and keep playing: the marble had been caching its own "locked" flag from the `LevelLost` signal, so it could fall out of step with the real game state. Fixed by exposing `GameStateManager.IsEnded` and having the marble check it directly for both input and `Die()`, so a respawn can no longer bypass the end of the level, and by having the win/lose/timer scripts unsubscribe from the autoload's signals on `_ExitTree` so a Retry doesn't leave stale handlers behind. Like the checkpoint and win-state work, this reaches into Andrew's timer/lose scope and Jayden's UI scope, so it's built minimally and flagged for their review. Developed on `feature/lose-state`.

## Week 3 (1–7 Oct 2026)

- **Lighting, rendering and gallery backdrop** — first visual-direction pass toward a bright, high-key "museum gallery" look. Added a shared `Gallery.tres` environment (`resources/environments/`) on a `WorldEnvironment` in `Main.tscn`: a warm off-white procedural sky (`GallerySky.tres`) as the source of ambient and reflected light, SSAO for contact shadows, SSR, subtle glow, and exponential fog with height fog so the backdrop below the play area fades into haze. Re-angled the `DirectionalLight3D` from straight down to a warm, low (~40°) sun with shadows enabled, so the arches cast long diagonal shadows across the tiles. Diagnosed why shadows were missing or blocky in-game but sharp in the editor: the orthographic camera sat 120 units back, beyond the directional shadow's default 100 m range, and even once in range, PSSM's perspective-oriented split spacing put the far half of the screen in a coarse split. Fixed by pulling the camera `Distance` in to 15 (no framing change under orthographic projection, since `OrthographicSize` controls zoom) and tightening shadow max distance, bias and pancake size. Tried orthogonal shadow mode, evenly spaced splits and PCSS soft shadows, but each introduced acne or over-softened the thin arch shadows, so kept default PSSM splits. Built a modular backdrop (`scenes/environment/`): `Column`, `Column_Big` and `Arch` CSG pieces sharing a `WhiteStone` material, assembled with the statue props into `Backdrop.tscn` placed below and behind the level, and hid the statue instances previously placed inside `Level01`. Developed on `feature/lighting-backdrop`.

## Week 4 (8–14 Oct 2026)

- **Camera-relative marble controls** — changed `MarbleController` so movement input is mapped relative to the active camera rather than world axes. Under the camera rig's fixed 45° isometric yaw, world-axis input made `W` roll the marble diagonally up-left on screen; it now takes the current `Camera3D`'s forward and right vectors, flattens them onto the ground plane (so the camera's downward pitch doesn't push the marble into the floor), and builds the force from those, so `W`/`S`/`A`/`D` match up/down/left/right on screen at any camera yaw. Reads the camera from the viewport rather than referencing `CameraRig`, so the controller stays decoupled from the camera system, and falls back to world axes when there's no camera (e.g. the marble scene run on its own). Developed on `feature/camera-relative-input`.
- **Camera zoom levels** — added five zoom levels (three zoomed in, regular, zoomed out) to `CameraRig`, stepped with the mouse wheel or Q/E via new `zoom_in`/`zoom_out` Input Map actions, with the camera easing between levels using the same frame-rate-independent exponential smoothing as the follow. The existing `OrthographicSize`/`Distance` exports define the regular level, so existing scene values keep their framing. Scaled the camera's distance with the zoom rather than changing only the orthographic size: zoomed out, a fixed distance would have put the near edge of the screen behind the camera and clipped it; zoomed in, moving closer keeps directional shadows sharp. Zoom input is read in `_UnhandledInput` so scrolling over menus doesn't also zoom the camera. Developed on `feature/camera-zoom`.
- **Background music system** — added a `MusicPlayer` autoload (`scripts/Audio/`) that owns all background music, and a `MusicCue` node that any scene can include to name its track: the menu cues Satie's *Gymnopédie No. 1*, Level 1 cues Debussy's *Arabesque No. 1*. Placing the player above the scene tree means music carries on through a Retry reload instead of restarting, and requesting the track that's already playing is ignored for the same reason; changing scenes crossfades between two players with a tween. It deliberately doesn't subscribe to win/lose, so the level music keeps playing behind the win and game over screens. Tracks are forced to loop in code so new files work without touching import settings, and the player keeps running while the tree is paused. Verified headlessly that the menu-to-level crossfade, Retry and Win behave as intended. Recorded the four Musopen tracks, with performers from their file tags, in [credits.md](credits.md). Audio is Jayden's scope (project-plan-responsibilities.md §1), so this is built as a base for Jayden's audio pass and flagged for Jayden's review. Developed on `feature/background-music`.
- **Marble rolling sound** — added `MarbleRollSound`, a looping `AudioStreamPlayer` on the marble whose volume and pitch follow how fast it's rolling, silent while airborne or still. It only reads its parent's physics state, keeping audio out of `MarbleController` as requirements.md §4 asks. Ground contact comes from a short downward ray using the marble's own collision mask, and only speed *along* the hit surface counts. The first version used total speed, which made every landing produce a burst of rolling sound; projecting onto the surface normal fixed that while still counting rolling down ramps. Kept it non-positional, because a 3D player would get quieter as the camera zoomed out. The loop runs continuously and only its volume changes, avoiding start/stop clicks. Trialled two clips. The first was a single 0.77 s roll that faded within itself, so it pulsed when looped. Switched to a 5 s CC0 recording (Freesound #661650), looping only its steady 1.4–4.0 s section via import loop points chosen at matching levels. It was recorded about 12 dB quieter than the music, so it's boosted +6 dB, the most its click peaks allow without clipping. Verified with a headless input simulation (landing silent, volume/pitch rising with speed, silent off the edge). Developed on `feature/rolling-sound`.
- **Marble landing sound** — added `MarbleImpactSound`, a "clunk" on the marble that plays when it goes from airborne to touching the ground. Its volume scales with the speed the marble was moving *into* the surface, with a small random pitch spread so repeated bounces don't sound identical. Defining a landing as an airborne-to-ground transition, rather than any sudden change in velocity, means rolling over ramp edges and tile seams (which change direction sharply without leaving the ground) stays silent, while each bounce after a drop gets its own quieter clunk. Respawning zeroes the marble's velocity in a single step, which read as a hard landing at the checkpoint, so the sound listens to the marble's existing `Died` signal and discards the stale velocity. Moved the ground raycast and radius lookup out of `MarbleRollSound` into a shared `MarbleGroundCheck` helper so both sounds use one definition of "on the ground". Verified headlessly: the start drop and its bounce clunk at decreasing volume, rolling across the level is silent, and a respawn produces no false clunk. Clip: CC0 (Freesound #240313). Developed on `feature/landing-sound`.
- **Well hazard model optimisation** — measured the triangle counts of the project's models and found the "decimated" hazard models were still about 1M+ triangles each (the well 1.25M, Severus 1.0M), roughly 10× the statue props, likely because the Decimate modifier hadn't been applied before export. Re-decimated the well in Blender to 37,585 triangles (−97%, 41.8 MB → 1.9 MB) and swapped it in under the same path and import UID, so `vacuum_hazard.tscn` needed no changes. Before swapping, confirmed the new mesh's bounds matched the old one to within about 2 cm, so the hazard's 0.3 scale and its collision cylinder still fit. Severus is still at 1M triangles and is next.

## Week 5 (15–21 Oct 2026)

- **Knock-over vase props** — turned seven imported vase models into physics props that can be dropped into any level (`scenes/props/vases/`). They stand still until the marble hits them, then topple and take some speed off it. Shared tuning lives on one base scene (`VaseBase.tscn`) that every vase inherits, with a reusable `KnockableProp` script. Measured each model and recentred it on its base, since the exported files carried their Blender layout offsets, and generated a convex-hull collision shape per vase design so colour variants share one shape. Headless testing found plain physics didn't produce a satisfying topple. Godot's automatic centre of mass sat at the shape's origin, the very base, so fallen vases swung back upright like weebles; fixed by computing a custom centre of mass at 45% of the shape's height. The marble also strikes low on the rounded body, which slid vases or tipped them back onto it. Added a push high on the vase in the marble's direction of travel, scaled by its speed and detected by a slightly enlarged trigger zone so the speed is read before the bounce rather than after. Tuned mass, damping and push from measured results: at a normal 4 m/s every vase topples, travels about 2 m and settles within about 6 s, while the marble keeps about 60% of its speed. Gentle bumps only shove the wider vases, and props that fall off the level are freed. Credited the vase pack, including its no-redistribution terms.
